Unit-Wide Employee Wellness Walkathon

Co-led a 300+ person wellness initiative across the CRM unit.

Overview

I conceptualized and led a unit-wide employee wellness initiative called Walkathon, designed to encourage healthier lifestyles and increase physical activity among employees across the CRM unit, which comprised 300+ employees.

The core objective was to motivate employees to become more physically active by encouraging them to work toward a 10,000-step daily target, while creating a sense of friendly competition, teamwork, and engagement across the unit.

The Challenge

Running a wellness program at this scale was a significant organisational challenge:

  • 300+ participants spread across multiple teams and functions.
  • Only two organisers — myself and one peer — responsible for the entire initiative.
  • Sustaining engagement over a three-month period, which is where most wellness programs lose momentum.
  • No dedicated budget or platform — everything had to be coordinated using existing tools and internal communication channels.

What I Did

I co-led the initiative end-to-end, from initial concept through to final recognition and rewards. My responsibilities spanned planning, execution, coordination, and program management.

Program Structure

We organised the 300+ participants into five teams, each led by a designated team leader responsible for motivating their members and encouraging participation throughout the initiative.

Fitness Tracking & Progress Monitoring

We integrated all participants into a fitness-tracking application, which allowed us to monitor individual and team step counts and track progress throughout the program.

Sustaining Engagement

To maintain momentum over the three-month initiative, we:

  • Coordinated regularly with team leaders.
  • Shared weekly progress updates across the unit.
  • Communicated achievements and milestones.
  • Used emails and internal communications to keep employees motivated and involved.
  • Created posters and communication campaigns to drive awareness and participation.

Recognition & Rewards

Beyond tracking steps, we structured the initiative around friendly competition and recognition, giving participants a tangible incentive to remain active:

  • Top-performing team — recognised with a team gift.
  • Top three individual participants — received T-shirts and recognition through the company's spotlight/reward points system, redeemable for gift cards and other rewards.

Results & Impact

  • 300+ employees engaged across the CRM unit.
  • Three months of sustained participation — a rarity for wellness programs of this scale.
  • Five competing teams driving friendly competition and cross-team collaboration.
  • Increased daily physical activity — employees worked consistently toward the 10,000-step target.
  • Delivered with just two organisers — requiring significant ownership, coordination, and communication.

Key Learnings

This initiative taught me that engagement at scale is fundamentally a communication problem. The structure — teams, leaders, weekly updates — mattered far more than any single incentive. I also learned that running something this large with only two people forces you to delegate meaningfully: the team leaders became force multipliers, and trusting them was essential. Most of all, it reinforced that people respond well to programs that are simple, visible, and social.
